The Curriculum

Four modules. Twelve weeks. The base your experiments run against.

Ikigai, the business case, and mission and values are not a warm-up — they are the information layer every experiment is measured against. The experiment system is the engine that turns that base into revenue and learning.

01
Module One
Week 1

Ikigai — founder–startup fit

The alignment check between you and the company you are building — where what you love, what you are good at, what the world needs, and what you can be paid for actually overlap, and whether this startup sits in that overlap.

The process
  1. Run a personality assessment and life planning session with a life coach.
  2. Map the four intersections — passion, profession, mission, vocation.
  3. Test the company against the map: is this the right thing, built by the right founder.
02
Module Two
Weeks 2–3

The business case

Why this company should exist and how it makes money. Not a plan you defend — a case you can test, with assumptions explicit enough to be proven wrong.

The process
  1. Assess your market with a business coach — size, trends, and where the real opportunity sits.
  2. Map your positioning against the competition — where you fit and how you differ.
  3. Build the revenue model and capital requirements — how the business makes money and what it takes to get there.
03
Module Three
Weeks 4–5

Mission and values

The deep purpose behind your business, and the values that guide how you run it. The mission decides which experiments are worth your weeks; the values decide how you run them under pressure.

The process
  1. Draft the mission statement — the change you exist to make, in plain language.
  2. Define the values as behaviors, not adjectives — what they look like on a hard day.
  3. Use them as the filter for what you say yes and no to in the experiment phase.
04
Module Four
Weeks 6–12

The experiment system

Finding traction means finding what is true by testing it — so you build the machine that reaches customers, captures what you learn, and books revenue.

The process
  1. Design experiments from your riskiest assumptions — each with a clear pass or fail.
  2. Build the infrastructure: personas, campaigns, outreach, and a live website.
  3. Talk to customers, record and summarize every conversation into a knowledge base.

Frequently Asked

Common questions about the Product Market Fit Workshop

Who is this workshop for?

Founders before product-market fit who are ready to stop guessing and start testing. You do not need a finished product or revenue — you need a company you are serious about and the willingness to talk to customers every week.

The workshop is deliberately small, 5 to 7 founders, so both the group session and your private hour stay high-signal.

What does the weekly commitment look like?

Two touchpoints a week: one group session with everyone in the workshop on the Waya OS modules, and one private hour on your specific path to PMF. Between sessions you run experiments, talk to customers, and build out your infrastructure — expect real work outside the calls.

What is Waya OS?

Waya OS is the founder operating system you set up during the workshop — the Ikigai, business case, mission and values, and experiment modules, delivered as guided facilitation inside your workspace. The method supplies the structure; the workshop walks you through running it against your own company.

Why start with Ikigai, business case, and mission and values instead of just running experiments?

Because those three are what make an experiment worth running and keep you honest about the result. Without them, founders test the wrong things and misread the answers. The base is the information layer; the experiments run against it.

Who runs the sessions?

Na'ama and Vince. One leads the weekly group session; one of them takes your private hour each week. Both are operators who have built, scaled, and rebuilt companies — you are working with founders, not a curriculum on autopilot.

What will I actually have built by the end?

A running reach-out engine — personas, campaigns, outreach, and a live website — a knowledge base that captures every customer conversation, booked calls and revenue, and an experiment loop you keep running after the workshop. The point is a working machine, not a deck.

What does it cost, and when does it start?

$3,000 for the full 12-week workshop. The first workshop starts the second week of November 2026. Seats are limited and filled by application.

How do I join?

Apply through the button above. Because the workshop is small and founder fit matters, enrollment runs through a short application and a conversation before you are in.
