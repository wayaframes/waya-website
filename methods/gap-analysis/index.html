Gap Analysis: What It Is and How to Do One

Gap analysis measures the distance between a current result and its target, then finds what is causing it.

Home › Methods › Gap Analysis

By Vincent Matranga, co-founder of Waya · Updated November 17, 2026

Gap analysis is a method for comparing where a result is today with where it needs to be, and finding the causes of the difference. It has three parts: state the problem as a measured, observable fact; choose an analysis method that fits the problem; and trace the gap to root causes you can act on.

What is gap analysis?

Gap analysis starts from a measured shortfall, such as a margin of 20% against a plan of 50%, and works out why it exists. The gap is the distance between the current condition and the target condition. The analysis explains that distance.

The term is used in two ways. In strategy and compliance work it often means a list of differences between a current and a desired state. In Lean problem solving it means the diagnostic step that explains a gap: step 4 of A3 thinking. This page covers the second meaning, which includes root cause analysis as its final part.

When should you use gap analysis?

Use gap analysis when something is underperforming and nobody can yet say why in a way that others accept. Typical cases:

  • A number is off target and the explanations are opinions.
  • The problem is described as a missing solution: "we need better training".
  • Several problems are being discussed as one.
  • A team is about to spend money on a fix.

What are the steps of a gap analysis?

A gap analysis has four steps, with a check between each one. Most of the value is in the first step. A problem that is stated well is usually half diagnosed.

  1. Solidify the problemWhat is happening that should not be?

    Turn the complaint into one sentence about what you can observe: what happens, how often, what it costs, and since when. Leave solutions and blame out of it.

  2. Choose the methodDoes the problem sit in one area or cross several?

    A problem inside one team suits 5 Whys. A problem that crosses teams suits a fishbone diagram. A problem that spans several processes may need a process map first.

  3. Run the analysisWhy does the gap exist?

    Trace the gap back cause by cause, with evidence for each link, until you reach causes you can act on.

  4. Check the root causesAre these good enough to act on?

    Each root cause should be backed by evidence and within your control. Rank them by effect on the gap. The top one or two go forward as experiments.

What makes a good problem statement?

A good problem statement describes what is happening in terms anyone could verify. It follows this pattern: a specific observable behavior is happening at this frequency, causing this measurable effect on these people, since this date. It should pass six tests.

TestWhat it means
SpecificClear about what is and is not part of the problem
MeasurableHas a number, a count, or a direct observation behind it
ActionableSomething the company could realistically address
RelevantLarge enough to be worth the time to analyze
Time-boundSays when it happens or when it started
ProvableRests on observed facts, with assumptions left out

Six patterns show that a problem statement is not ready. Each has a fix.

Which analysis method fits the problem?

The method depends first on scope: whether the problem lives in one area or crosses several. Information and people settle the rest.

What does a gap analysis look like? A worked example

This example comes from Cheetah, the food distributor Waya's founders ran, after it acquired three regional distributors. The full account is in the case study A post-acquisition turnaround.

StageWhat happened
The gapFill rate fell to about 75%. Shrink ran at 17–18% for the month. On-time delivery fell as low as 24%, and about 22% of orders shipped with an error. The operation was burning about $1M a month in contribution margin.
The first explanationThe warehouse crews.
What the gap analysis foundThe warehouse had been physically rebuilt, with new racking, a new freezer, and hundreds of new locations, and the warehouse management system's logic was never updated. Replenishment sent product to slots too small to hold it and scattered single items across as many as 886 locations. There had also been no full inventory count of the acquired businesses.
The fixesTwo tracks at once. Daily manual triage to clear the backlog. A rebuilt slotting model in the system, re-zoning to the new racking, and barcode scanning with hard stops, written into daily standard work.
The resultShrink fell to about 2%. Fill rate rose above 99%. On-time delivery rose to about 90%. Orders with an error fell to about 3%. The operation was profitable by October 2023.

Adding labor would have treated the symptom. The system logic was undoing the crews' work as fast as they did it.

What mistakes do teams make in gap analysis?

  • Asking what should be happening too early. That question invites solutions. Stay with what is happening until the problem statement is settled.
  • Challenging every number. Check the figures the conclusion rests on, and let minor details go.
  • Forcing precision on a broken process. When nobody follows a standard, "it varies" is the accurate description. Record it and move on.
  • Splitting one process into several problems. Receiving, updating, and tracking a ticket is one flow. Analyze it as one.
  • Polishing the statement. The standard is good enough to act on.

How do you run a gap analysis in a small company?

A small company can run a gap analysis in about an hour, and should hold a lighter standard of evidence than a large one.

  • Observation is evidence. "Our tracking showed a backlog" is enough to proceed. Speed matters more than statistical rigor.
  • One or two questions at a time. A founder working alone should still write each answer down before moving to the next question.
  • Stop when you can act. The analysis is done when the cause is clear enough to test a fix.

Can an AI assistant run a gap analysis?

An AI assistant can facilitate a gap analysis, and the first step is where it helps most. A general assistant accepts "we need better training" and starts designing the training. Given the method, it asks what gap the training would close, turns the answer into a problem statement, and suggests 5 Whys or a fishbone diagram to match the scope.

Lean AI is Waya's MCP server that runs gap analysis this way inside Claude. The observations and the numbers still come from you.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between gap analysis and root cause analysis?

Root cause analysis is the part of a gap analysis that traces the causes. Gap analysis starts earlier: it first turns a vague complaint into a measured problem statement and chooses the right analysis method, then runs the root cause analysis.

How long does a gap analysis take?

Between 45 and 90 minutes for most problems: about 20 minutes to state the problem, 10 to choose the method, and the rest to trace the causes. A problem that crosses several teams takes longer because more people need to be in the room.

Do you need data to do a gap analysis?

You need evidence, and in a small or fast-growing company direct observation counts. "We compared several purchase orders with what should have been bought, and about one in ten was different" is enough to start. Check the numbers that the conclusion depends on.

What comes after a gap analysis?

Experiments. Each root cause becomes a hypothesis: if we change this, we expect that result. You test the change, measure the result against the target, and keep what works.

Is gap analysis the same as a SWOT analysis?

No. A SWOT analysis lists strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats to inform strategy. A gap analysis starts from one measured shortfall and finds its causes.
